## 6. Candidate Proposals

### Candidate 1: CAND-101 — Path-Dependent Sequence Asymmetry

**Expression Class:** STATE / CONDITION
**Mechanism Family:** Path-Dependent Sequencing

**Market phenomenon:** The ORDER in which structural events (breaks, retests) occur creates distinct market conditions. A break followed by a retest (B→R) represents a different participant dynamic than a retest followed by a break (R→B).

**Mechanism:** Participants interpret event sequences as signals. B→R is interpreted as "break confirmed by successful retest" (conviction). R→B is interpreted as "level failed after testing" (trap). These interpretations produce different positioning responses, creating distinct downstream economics.

**Observable:** Event sequence type (B→R vs R→B) and inter-event timing.

**Expected economic effect:** B→R sequences produce tighter distributions (conviction, orderly repricing). R→B sequences produce wider distributions (trap, forced exits).

**Why this is genuinely distinct from prior work:**
- CAND-091 tested cumulative directional exhaustion (N same-direction moves → exhaustion). CAND-101 tests event ORDER, not count.
- CAND-098 tested event cluster density (number of events in window). CAND-101 tests event SEQUENCE, not density.
- CAND-092 tested time-since-single-event. CAND-101 tests multi-event sequences.
- 14 Event Sequence candidates tested event detection, not sequence structure.

### Candidate 2: CAND-102 — Directional Momentum Persistence

**Expression Class:** STATE / CONDITION
**Mechanism Family:** Directional Dynamics

**Market phenomenon:** After a series of consecutive same-direction structural breaks, the market develops a directional bias. This is the persistence hypothesis — the OPPOSITE of CAND-091's exhaustion hypothesis.

**Mechanism:** When multiple breaks occur in the same direction, participants who are positioned against the trend face increasing pressure. Each additional same-direction break forces more participants to capitulate and align with the trend. The cumulative effect is momentum reinforcement, not exhaustion. The market "learns" the direction through repeated confirmation.

**Observable:** Count of consecutive same-direction structural breaks (run length). Direction of next break.

**Expected economic effect:** Higher run lengths produce stronger directional persistence — the next break is more likely to continue the same direction. The economic effect is directional bias, not distribution width.

**Why this is genuinely distinct from prior work:**
- CAND-091 tested EXHAUSTION: cumulative directional moves → reversal. CAND-102 tests PERSISTENCE: cumulative directional moves → continuation.
- These are opposite hypotheses about the same observable class. CAND-091 predicted exhaustion after N same-direction moves. CAND-102 predicts persistence.
- No QuantForge candidate has tested directional PERSISTENCE as a market mechanism.
- The CAND-091 result (hypothesis contradicted — exhaustion not found) does not automatically validate persistence. Persistence must be independently tested.

## 7. Mechanism–Observable Challenge

### CAND-101: Path-Dependent Sequence Asymmetry

#### 7.1 Mechanism Independence

**What market phenomenon exists independently of the proposed feature?**

Market participants interpret sequences of price actions as signals. When price breaks a level and then returns to test it, this is interpreted differently from when price tests a level and then breaks through it. These interpretations exist independently of any specific formula — they are fundamental to how traders read price action. The phenomenon is: sequence-dependent interpretation of price action.

#### 7.2 Proxy Challenge

**How could the observable arise WITHOUT the claimed mechanism?**

The sequence type (B→R vs R→B) could arise mechanically from price dynamics without any participant interpretation. For example, B→R could occur simply because price overshoots a level and mean-reverts, while R→B could occur because price fails to break a level and then eventually accumulates enough energy to break. These are mechanical price dynamics, not participant interpretation.

#### 7.3 Alternative Explanation

**What competing market process could create the same observable?**

1. **Mechanical overshoot/mean-reversion:** B→R could reflect simple overshoot dynamics rather than participant conviction.
2. **Accumulation dynamics:** R→B could reflect slow accumulation of buying/selling pressure rather than trap dynamics.
3. **Volatility clustering:** Both sequences could be more common during volatile periods, creating a spurious association with downstream economics.

#### 7.4 Observable Fidelity

**Why is the observable a reasonable measurement of the mechanism?**

The sequence type (B→R vs R→B) directly captures the ORDER of events, which is the core of the hypothesis. However, the mechanism (participant interpretation) is inferred, not directly observed. The observable captures the structural pattern; the mechanism explains WHY the pattern matters.

#### 7.5 Expected-Effect Bridge

**Why should the mechanism alter the subsequent outcome distribution?**

If participants interpret B→R as conviction and R→B as trap, then:
- B→R should produce more orderly subsequent price action (participants are aligned, less forced positioning)
- R→B should produce more disorderly subsequent price action (participants are trapped, forced exits create noise)
This creates different distribution shapes for the two sequence types.

#### 7.6 Falsification

**What result would clearly contradict the hypothesis?**

If B→R and R→B sequences produce identical downstream distributions (no difference in mean, median, standard deviation, or win rate), the hypothesis is contradicted. If R→B produces TIGHTER distributions than B→R (opposite of predicted), the mechanism explanation is wrong (though the observable might still be informative in the unexpected direction).

#### 7.7 Tradeability Challenge

**What realistic costs or execution constraints could eliminate the effect?**

1. **Timing:** Identifying the sequence type requires waiting for both events, which may delay entry.
2. **Frequency:** If B→R and R→B sequences are rare, sample sizes may be insufficient.
3. **Transaction costs:** If the separation is small (<2 bps), costs consume the effect.
4. **Parameter sensitivity:** If the result depends heavily on the specific break/retest definitions, it may not be robust.

#### 7.8 Base-Rate Challenge

**Given the historical lack of G2 promotions, what specifically makes this candidate worth spending G1 resources on?**

This candidate tests a genuinely unexplored mechanism: event ORDER as a conditioning variable. 14 Event Sequence candidates have been registered, but all tested event detection or count, not sequence structure. The base rate for event-sequence research is poor, but this candidate addresses a specific blind spot (order effects) that has never been tested. The Observable Information Potential is rated separately from Mechanism Confidence.

**Proxy Confidence:** MODERATE — The sequence type is observable, but the mechanism (participant interpretation) is inferred.
**Mechanism Confidence:** MODERATE — The economic logic is plausible, but alternative mechanical explanations exist.
**Observable Information Potential:** MODERATE — The sequence type could encode information even if the mechanism explanation is incomplete.

### CAND-102: Directional Momentum Persistence

#### 7.1 Mechanism Independence

**What market phenomenon exists independently of the proposed feature?**

Directional momentum is a well-documented market phenomenon. Prices that have been moving in one direction tend to continue in that direction over short horizons. This is independent of any specific formula — it is a statistical property of financial markets. The specific mechanism proposed here is that structural breaks (not just returns) exhibit persistence: consecutive same-direction breaks create directional bias.

#### 7.2 Proxy Challenge

**How could the observable arise WITHOUT the claimed mechanism?**

The run-length of same-direction breaks could arise from:
1. **Trending markets:** In a trending market, breaks naturally occur in the same direction. The run length reflects the trend, not a persistence mechanism.
2. **Volatility clustering:** High-volatility periods produce more breaks, which may be directional by chance.
3. **Look-ahead bias:** If break definitions are sensitive to future data, the run length could be an artifact.

#### 7.3 Alternative Explanation

**What competing market process could create the same observable?**

1. **Simple trend persistence:** Prices that have been going up continue going up. This is a well-known statistical property that does not require a specific structural-break mechanism.
2. **Volatility regime:** High-volatility periods produce more breaks in the same direction simply because the market is moving more.
3. **Sample composition:** If most of the data is from a trending period, same-direction breaks dominate by construction.

#### 7.4 Observable Fidelity

**Why is the observable a reasonable measurement of the mechanism?**

The run length of same-direction structural breaks directly measures the core hypothesis: how many consecutive breaks have occurred in the same direction. However, the mechanism (participant capitulation / momentum reinforcement) is inferred. The observable captures the structural pattern; the mechanism explains why it matters.

#### 7.5 Expected-Effect Bridge

**Why should the mechanism alter the subsequent outcome distribution?**

If same-direction breaks create momentum reinforcement:
- Higher run lengths should produce stronger directional continuation
- The next break should be more likely to continue the same direction
- The DIRECTIONAL bias should increase with run length
This is a directional hypothesis, not a distributional one.

#### 7.6 Falsification

**What result would clearly contradict the hypothesis?**

If same-direction runs produce NO directional bias (next break direction is random regardless of run length), the hypothesis is contradicted. If same-direction runs produce REVERSAL bias (next break is more likely to go opposite), the EXHAUSTION hypothesis is supported instead, and this candidate's mechanism is wrong.

#### 7.7 Tradeability Challenge

**What realistic costs or execution constraints could eliminate the effect?**

1. **Timing:** Identifying the run length requires observing multiple prior breaks, which delays the signal.
2. **Frequency:** Long runs (3+) may be rare, limiting opportunities.
3. **Transaction costs:** If the directional bias is small, costs consume the edge.
4. **Regime dependence:** The effect may exist only in trending periods, not range-bound periods.

#### 7.8 Base-Rate Challenge

**Given the historical lack of G2 promotions, what specifically makes this candidate worth spending G1 resources on?**

CAND-091 tested directional EXHAUSTION and found the hypothesis contradicted (no exhaustion effect). The OPPOSITE hypothesis — directional PERSISTENCE — has never been tested. If CAND-091 found no exhaustion, it is scientifically important to test whether the market instead exhibits persistence. This candidate directly tests the complement of a known result. The base rate is poor, but the scientific question is precise and the observable is clean.

**Proxy Confidence:** MODERATE — The run length is observable, but the mechanism (momentum reinforcement) is inferred from general market knowledge.
**Mechanism Confidence:** MODERATE — Momentum persistence is a well-known market phenomenon, but applying it specifically to structural breaks is novel.
**Observable Information Potential:** HIGH — Directional run lengths are a clean observable that could encode information even if the specific mechanism is wrong.

## 11. Temporal-Semantic Audit

### CAND-101: Path-Dependent Sequence Asymmetry

```
ANCHOR: Sequence of two structural events (break + retest, or retest + break)
OBSERVATION WINDOW: The sequence itself (variable length depending on inter-event timing)
OUTCOME WINDOW: H bars after the SECOND event in the sequence
```

**Leakage check:** No future information enters the sequence classification. Both events must have occurred before the outcome window begins. The sequence type is determined by the ORDER of two past events.

**Overlap handling:** If multiple sequences overlap, each is treated as an independent observation. The outcome window starts after the second event.

**Deduplication:** Same sequence type at the same level within a short window is counted once.

### CAND-102: Directional Momentum Persistence

```
ANCHOR: A structural break that extends a same-direction run to length N
OBSERVATION WINDOW: The N consecutive same-direction breaks (variable length)
OUTCOME WINDOW: H bars after the Nth break
```

**Leakage check:** The run length is computed from past breaks only. No future information enters the run-length calculation.

**Overlap handling:** If the outcome window overlaps with the next break, the observation is included but the outcome window is truncated at the next break.

**Deduplication:** Each break that extends a run is counted once as a new observation with the updated run length.

## 13. Tradeability Challenge

2. Long runs (N≥3) must occur frequently enough (≥200 over the data period)

- Long runs are rare, limiting opportunities
- The directional bias is small and consumed by costs
- Simple trend persistence explains the entire effect (no incremental information)
