### MECH-F01: FAILED BREAKOUT INVENTORY REVERSAL

**Mechanism ID:** MECH-F01

**Mechanism name:** Failed Breakout Inventory Reversal

#### Market process

When price breaks a structurally significant level (a recent high or low), breakout traders enter in the breakout direction and contrarian traders enter against it. If the breakout subsequently FAILS — price reverses back through the breakout level — both groups are trapped: breakout traders hold losing positions in the wrong direction, and contrarian traders hold profitable positions. The trapped breakout traders must liquidate (stop-losses trigger, margin calls force exits). This forced liquidation creates mechanical selling pressure (for a failed upside breakout) or buying pressure (for a failed downside breakout) that pushes price further in the reversal direction. The forced liquidation is the mechanism — it is not "momentum continued" or "mean reversion." It is a specific participant class (trapped breakout traders) being forced to act by position management rules.

#### Participant interpretation

- **Breakout traders (trapped):** Entered at the breakout expecting continuation. When the breakout fails, they are underwater. Stop-losses and margin rules force liquidation. The liquidation is mechanical, not discretionary.
- **Contrarian traders (profitable):** Sold the breakout (or bought the downside breakout). When the breakout fails, they are profitable. Some may take profits (reducing the reversal force), but others may add to positions (increasing the reversal force).
- **Range-bound participants:** Were holding positions within the pre-breakout range. The breakout attempt and failure may have stopped some out; the reversal may bring them back.
- **Market makers:** Adjusted quotes for the breakout; must adjust again for the failure. The double adjustment creates additional flow.

The key insight: the FAILED BREAKOUT is a distinct event from a successful breakout. The participant behavior after failure is mechanically different from participant behavior after success.

#### Observable manifestation

At M1 resolution:
- Bar E: price breaks a structural level (close above recent high or below recent low) — the "breakout bar"
- Bars E+1 through E+F: price reverses and closes back through the breakout level — the "failure confirmation"
- Bar E+F: the failure is confirmed (price has closed back through the level) — the "failure event"
- Direction: failed upside breakout → SHORT bias (trapped longs must liquidate); failed downside breakout → LONG bias (trapped shorts must liquidate)

The observable is deterministic: compare each bar's close to the breakout level. The failure is confirmed when a close occurs back through the level.

#### State/event transition

The transition is from "breakout in progress" to "breakout failed." This is a discrete event at bar E+F, not a continuous variable. Before E+F, the breakout may still succeed. After E+F, the breakout has failed and trapped participants must adjust.

#### Potential economic consequence

After failure confirmation:
- Trapped breakout traders liquidate positions (forced selling for failed upside, forced buying for failed downside)
- The forced liquidation creates directional pressure in the reversal direction
- The pressure is mechanical (driven by position management rules, not discretion)
- The economic consequence occurs at a tradeable horizon (immediately after failure confirmation) because the liquidation is triggered by the failure event itself

#### Directional consequence

- Failed upside breakout → SHORT (trapped longs liquidate → selling pressure)
- Failed downside breakout → LONG (trapped shorts liquidate → buying pressure)

The direction is OPPOSITE to the original breakout direction (fade the breakout after failure).

#### Falsification

The mechanism is falsified if:
- Failed breakouts and successful breakouts show identical subsequent economics
- The forced liquidation after failure does not create measurable directional pressure
- The reversal after failure is random (no directional predictability)
- Trapped participants do not behave as hypothesized (e.g., they hold through the loss without liquidating)

#### Data requirements

- M1 OHLCV for a single instrument (e.g., USATECHIDXUSD)
- Structural level definition (e.g., recent N-bar high/low) — frozen at registration
- Failure confirmation window (F bars) — frozen at registration
- Standard M1 data — no exotic data required

#### Execution feasibility

Can a future Base process act on the information? YES. The failure event is deterministic and observable in real time. Entry after failure confirmation, exit at session close. No hindsight required.

#### Distinctness

| Nearest prior mechanism | Why MECH-F01 is different |
|------------------------|--------------------------|
| BASE-001 (structural level validation) | BASE-001 follows VALIDATED breakouts (price holds beyond level for K bars). MECH-F01 follows FAILED breakouts (price reverses back through level). Different event (validation vs. failure), different direction (follow vs. fade), different participant behavior (new entrants vs. trapped liquidation). |
| MECH-N01 (structural level validation flow) | MECH-N01 is BASE-001's mechanism. MECH-F01 is the opposite event. N01 follows validated breakouts; F01 fades failed breakouts. Different mechanism entirely. |

### MECH-F02: SHOCK-INDUCED POSITION ADJUSTMENT CASCADE

**Mechanism ID:** MECH-F02

**Mechanism name:** Shock-Induced Position Adjustment Cascade

#### Market process

When an unusually large price move occurs (significantly larger than recent normal activity), it triggers mandatory position adjustments across multiple participant classes. Stop-losses trigger. Margin calls are issued. Risk limits are breached. Volatility-targeting strategies reduce exposure. These adjustments are mechanically driven — they follow from position management rules, not from discretionary information processing. The adjustments create additional directional flow in the same direction as the original move (stop-outs add selling pressure during a decline, buying pressure during an advance). This creates a self-reinforcing cascade: the large move → forced adjustments → additional directional flow → further price movement. The cascade eventually exhausts as the pool of forced participants is depleted. After exhaustion, the remaining participants are fundamentally motivated, and price may revert or stabilize.

#### Participant interpretation

- **Stop-loss participants:** Had positions with stops placed at specific levels. The large move triggers their stops. Stop execution adds directional flow (selling during decline, buying during advance).
- **Margin-called participants:** Hold positions that have exceeded margin requirements due to the large move. Forced liquidation adds directional flow.
- **Risk-limit participants:** Have position limits that are breached by the move's P&L impact. Forced reduction adds directional flow.
- **Volatility-targeting participants:** Target a specific portfolio volatility. The large move increases realized volatility, causing them to reduce exposure. Reduction adds directional flow (selling if long, buying if short).
- **Market makers:** Adjust hedging and inventory in response to the move. Adjustment adds additional flow.

The key insight: the LARGE MOVE itself creates a participant behavior cascade that is mechanically predictable. The cascade is not "momentum" — it is forced repositioning by participants whose position management rules are triggered by the move.

#### Observable manifestation

At M1 resolution:
- Bar E: price produces an unusually large directional move (close-to-close return exceeding a structural threshold S relative to recent volatility)
- Bars E+1 through E+L: price continues in the same direction as the forced adjustments create additional flow
- Bar E+L: the cascade exhausts (forced adjustments are complete)

The observable is deterministic: compare the bar's return to the recent volatility-based threshold. The cascade direction is the same as the original move.

#### State/event transition

The transition is from "normal market function" to "forced-adjustment cascade." This occurs when the large move exceeds the structural threshold S. After the threshold is breached, participant behavior changes mechanically. After the cascade exhausts, normal function resumes.

#### Potential economic consequence

After the large move triggers the cascade:
- Forced adjustments create additional directional flow
- The flow is in the same direction as the original move
- The flow persists until the pool of forced participants is depleted
- After exhaustion, remaining participants are fundamentally motivated

The economic consequence occurs at a tradeable horizon because the forced adjustments are mechanically triggered by the move and persist until the forced participant pool is exhausted.

#### Directional consequence

- Large upside move → LONG bias (forced buying from short stop-outs and margin calls)
- Large downside move → SHORT bias (forced selling from long stop-outs and margin calls)

The direction is the SAME as the original large move (follow the forced-adjustment flow).

#### Falsification

The mechanism is falsified if:
- Large moves do not produce continued directional flow in the same direction
- The continued flow is random (no directional predictability after large moves)
- Forced adjustments are not observable in subsequent price behavior
- The cascade exhaustion point is not structurally identifiable

#### Data requirements

- M1 OHLCV for a single instrument (e.g., USATECHIDXUSD)
- S (structural threshold for large move detection) — frozen at registration
- L (cascade observation window) — frozen at registration
- Standard M1 data — no exotic data required

#### Execution feasibility

Can a future Base process act on the information? YES. The large move is deterministic and observable in real time. Entry after cascade begins, exit after cascade exhausts or at session close. No hindsight required.

#### Distinctness

|------------------------|--------------------------|
| MECH-F01 (failed breakout inventory reversal) | MECH-F01 trades forced liquidation AFTER A FAILED BREAKOUT. MECH-F02 trades forced adjustments AFTER ANY LARGE MOVE. Different trigger (breakout failure vs. large move), different direction (fade the breakout vs. follow the large move), different participant class (trapped breakout traders vs. stop-out/margin-call participants). |
| BASE-001 (structural level validation) | BASE-001 follows validated breakouts of structural levels. MECH-F02 follows large moves regardless of structural levels. Different trigger, different mechanism. |
| Post-magnitude directional drift (exhausted) | Post-magnitude drift is about directional continuation after large moves. MECH-F02 is about the MECHANISM of forced adjustments creating the continuation. Different mechanism (forced repositioning vs. drift), different participant behavior. |

The trigger is different (large move threshold vs. trend direction). The mechanism is different (forced repositioning vs. trend following). |
| Volatility regime / state (exhausted) | Vol regime trades the level of volatility. MECH-F02 trades the FORCED ADJUSTMENTS triggered by a large move. Different mechanism. |

### MECH-F03: OVERNIGHT GAP INVENTORY REBALANCING

**Mechanism ID:** MECH-F03

**Mechanism name:** Overnight Gap Inventory Rebalancing

#### Market process

When US equity index markets close, information continues to accumulate through global futures trading, overnight news, and Asian/European sessions. This information cannot be incorporated into US equity index prices until the US market reopens. At the US market open, the opening price reflects a combination of: (a) the accumulated overnight information, and (b) the positioning imbalances created by the gap between the previous close and the new open. The gap itself creates a specific participant dynamic: holders who were long overnight and see the gap down must decide whether to hold or sell; holders who were short overnight and see the gap up must decide whether to cover or hold; gap traders enter based on the gap direction. The opening session (first N bars) sees a predictable resolution of these positioning imbalances. The resolution has a directional bias: if the gap reflects genuine information, the direction continues; if the gap reflects temporary positioning, the direction reverses. The mechanism is the INVENTORY REBALANCING at the open, not the gap itself.

#### Participant interpretation

- **Overnight holders (long):** Held long positions overnight. If the gap is down, they are underwater. Some will sell at the open (creating selling pressure). Others will hold (reducing selling pressure). The balance determines the opening flow.
- **Overnight holders (short):** Held short positions overnight. If the gap is up, they are underwater. Some will cover at the open (creating buying pressure). Others will hold (reducing buying pressure).
- **Gap traders:** Enter based on the gap direction. If the gap is up, some buy (continuation). If the gap is down, some sell (continuation). Their flow adds to the opening direction.
- **Market makers:** Adjusted quotes for the gap. Must adjust inventory at the open. The adjustment adds flow.
- **New session entrants:** Participants who only trade during US market hours. They enter based on the overnight information reflected in the gap. Their flow adds to the opening direction.

The key insight: the GAP is not the mechanism. The INVENTORY REBALANCING at the open is the mechanism. The gap creates the positioning imbalance; the rebalancing resolves it.

#### Observable manifestation

At M1 resolution:
- Bar E: US market close (previous session's last bar)
- Bar S: US market open (current session's first bar). The gap = open of S - close of E.
- Bars S through S+N: opening session directional movement
- The gap size and direction are deterministic (open - previous close)
- The opening session direction is deterministic (compare subsequent bars to the open)

The observable is deterministic: the gap is `open_S - close_E`. The opening session direction is measured by subsequent bars' closes relative to the open.

#### State/event transition

The transition is from "overnight information accumulation" to "opening session rebalancing." This occurs at the US market open (bar S). Before S, information accumulates but cannot be traded. At S, the gap reflects the accumulated information and the positioning imbalance is created. During S through S+N, the rebalancing resolves the imbalance.

#### Potential economic consequence

After the gap opens:
- Overnight holders rebalance positions (creating directional flow)
- Gap traders enter (adding to directional flow)
- Market makers adjust inventory (adding to directional flow)
- The combined flow creates a directional bias in the opening session

The economic consequence occurs at a tradeable horizon (immediately after the open) because the inventory rebalancing is triggered by the gap and persists until the imbalance is resolved.

#### Directional consequence

- Gap up → LONG bias in opening session (short covering + gap-buying + continuation)
- Gap down → SHORT bias in opening session (long liquidation + gap-selling + continuation)

The direction is the SAME as the gap direction (follow the rebalancing flow).

#### Falsification

The mechanism is falsified if:
- Opening session direction is random relative to gap direction
- Gap size does not predict opening session magnitude
- Inventory rebalancing is not observable in opening session behavior
- The opening session shows no directional bias regardless of gap characteristics

#### Data requirements

- M1 OHLCV for a single instrument (e.g., USATECHIDXUSD)
- N (opening session measurement window) — frozen at registration
- Session boundary definition (US market open/close times) — frozen at registration
- Standard M1 data — no exotic data required

#### Execution feasibility

Can a future Base process act on the information? YES. The gap is deterministic and observable at the open. Entry at the open, exit at session close or after N bars. No hindsight required.

## 16. QUALITATIVE PRIORITIZATION

### Ranking dimensions

1. **Mechanism clarity** — How clearly can the mechanism be stated?
2. **Participant plausibility** — How plausible is the participant-behavior hypothesis?
3. **Observable determinism** — How deterministic is the observable?
4. **Prospective testability** — How easily can the mechanism be tested prospectively?
5. **Economic pathway** — How clear is the path from mechanism to economic consequence?
6. **Execution realism** — Can a future Base process act on the information?
7. **Falsification quality** — How clearly can the mechanism be falsified?
8. **Independence** — How distinct is this from all exhausted and closed research?
9. **Opportunity-population clarity** — How clearly defined is the eligible population?
10. **Formulation simplicity** — How simple is the eventual formulation?

### Adjudication

**MECH-F01 (Failed Breakout Inventory Reversal):**
- Mechanism clarity: HIGH — failed breakout → trapped participants → forced liquidation is concrete and mechanistic
- Participant plausibility: HIGH — stop-outs and forced liquidation are well-documented market phenomena
- Observable determinism: HIGH — breakout and failure are binary events, fully deterministic
- Prospective testability: HIGH — forward data will naturally produce breakout failures
- Economic pathway: HIGH — forced liquidation → directional pressure → economic consequence
- Execution realism: HIGH — entry after failure confirmation, exit at session close
- Falsification quality: HIGH — failed vs. successful breakouts can be directly compared
- Independence: HIGH — distinct from all exhausted dimensions, BASE-001, and relational work
- Opportunity clarity: HIGH — one opportunity per failed breakout
- Formulation simplicity: HIGH — simple binary events (breakout, failure), minimal parameters (N, F)

**MECH-F02 (Shock-Induced Position Adjustment Cascade):**
- Mechanism clarity: HIGH — large move → forced adjustments → cascade is concrete and mechanistic
- Participant plausibility: HIGH — stop-outs, margin calls, and risk-limit adjustments are well-documented
- Observable determinism: HIGH — large move detection is deterministic (threshold-based)
- Prospective testability: HIGH — forward data will naturally produce large moves
- Economic pathway: HIGH — forced adjustments → directional flow → economic consequence
- Execution realism: HIGH — entry after large move detected, exit after cascade exhausts
- Falsification quality: HIGH — large moves with continuation vs. without can be compared
- Independence: HIGH — distinct from all exhausted dimensions, BASE-001, and relational work
- Opportunity clarity: MEDIUM — one opportunity per large move, but threshold S defines "large"
- Formulation simplicity: MEDIUM — requires S and L parameters, but both are structurally definable

**MECH-F03 (Overnight Gap Inventory Rebalancing):**
- Mechanism clarity: HIGH — overnight gap → inventory rebalancing → opening session flow is concrete
- Participant plausibility: HIGH — overnight positioning and opening rebalancing are well-understood
- Observable determinism: HIGH — gap is deterministic (open - previous close)
- Prospective testability: HIGH — every trading day produces a gap (or no gap)
- Economic pathway: MEDIUM — inventory rebalancing → directional flow → economic consequence (but the link between rebalancing and continuation is inferential)
- Execution realism: HIGH — entry at open, exit after N bars or session close
- Falsification quality: HIGH — gap-up vs. gap-down opening sessions can be directly compared
- Independence: HIGH — distinct from all exhausted dimensions, BASE-001, and relational work
- Opportunity clarity: HIGH — one opportunity per trading day (gap or no gap)
- Formulation simplicity: HIGH — simple gap detection, minimal parameters (N for opening window)
