## 10. Repeated Failure Pattern

Across V19-V24, a clear and consistent pattern has emerged:

### Pattern A: Informative But Economically Flat (V20, V24)
Conditions that demonstrably change the distribution (treatment > counterfactual) but produce near-zero absolute returns. The informational value is real but too small to monetize.

### Pattern B: Counterfactual Superior (V19, V21, V23)
The registered control outperforms the treatment. The hypothesis is falsified or the confirmation condition actively destroys edge.

### Pattern C: Evidence-Limited / Zero-Event (V19, V20, V21, V22, V23)
The event is too rare to statistically adjudicate, even when the per-event economics might be large.

### Pattern D: Negative Expectancy (V21, V22, V23)
The treatment itself produces negative returns after friction.

**The dominant pattern is A+B:** Conditions with genuine microstructural information value that cannot survive retail friction. This is the Research Factory's systematic output for the last 6 cycles.

## 11. Economic Displacement Review

The V19-V24 candidates consistently tested:
- Session timing (open, close, lunch, mid-day)
- Structural geometry (sweeps, breakouts, retests, freshness)
- Sequential patterns (absorption, rejection, engulfing)
- Calendar structure (Friday range, weekly anchors)
- Cross-market correlation (Gold→Tech)

What they consistently did NOT test:
- Forced settlement/liquidation flows
- Mandatory benchmark tracking
- Contract expiry/roll pressure
- Explicit arbitrage dislocations
- Large asymmetric liquidity events
- Financing/carry constraints
- Mechanically constrained participant behavior

The candidates are testing **price patterns** rather than **economic displacement mechanisms**. The price patterns contain real information (treatment > counterfactual), but the information reflects microstructural noise that is too small to overcome friction.

**Assessment: POSSIBLE CONDITIONAL-VALUE BIAS**

Evidence supporting:
- 6 consecutive G0 cycles produced candidates that are informationally valid but economically unmonetizable
- The G0 template emphasizes condition filters, structural states, and relative differences
- Candidate generation gravitates toward session timing and price geometry
- No candidate from V19-V24 directly tests a forced-flow or settlement mechanism

Evidence against:
- V24 introduced cross-market transmission (genuinely new family)
- Each cycle rotates mechanism families as instructed
- The failure may reflect the reality of retail CFD markets (high friction kills marginal edges)
- Some cycles (V19, V23) produced counterfactual-superior falsifications, which is valid science

Uncertainty:
- The bias may be in G0 candidate generation OR in the inherent limitations of M1 price data for detecting economically meaningful displacement
- Without testing direct economic mechanisms (settlement, forced flow), we cannot distinguish between "G0 generates weak ideas" and "M1 data cannot capture strong ideas"

## 17. Recommended G0 Emphasis

Based on V19-V24 evidence, the next G0 cycle should:

1. **Prioritize direct economic displacement mechanisms** over conditional filters. Candidates should express: FORCED PARTICIPANT FLOW → LARGE PRICE CONSEQUENCE → EXECUTABLE ENTRY.

2. **Weight mechanically constrained behavior** more heavily. Settlement, benchmark tracking, contract expiry, and financing pressure create directional flows that are structural, not conditional.

3. **Reduce emphasis on** session timing, structural geometry, and sequential price patterns as primary Alpha sources. These may be valuable as state filters but have proven insufficient as standalone Alphas.

4. **Preserve rare-event research.** CAND-024 and CAND-035 demonstrate that rare, high-expectancy mechanisms exist and are worth pursuing through forward qualification.

5. **Consider whether M1 data is sufficient** for detecting economically meaningful displacement. Some mechanisms (settlement flow, benchmark tracking) may require higher-frequency data or different observables.
