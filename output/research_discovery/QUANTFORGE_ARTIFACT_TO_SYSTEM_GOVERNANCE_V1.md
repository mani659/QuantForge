# QUANTFORGE — ARTIFACT TO SYSTEM GOVERNANCE V1
# 2026-08-26

## 1. Purpose
To formally distinguish between the discovery of individual scientific edge components (Research Artifacts) and the construction of deployable trading architectures (Strategy Systems). This distinction ensures that scientifically valid, high-margin, but infrequently occurring market phenomena are not prematurely killed solely for lack of standalone trading frequency.

## 2. Research Artifact Definition
A scientifically and economically qualified market behavior (edge) that has survived the QuantForge rigorous screening process. An artifact represents a genuine conditional probability shift in the market, but it does NOT inherently need to trade frequently enough to constitute a complete, standalone, deployable strategy.

## 3. Standalone Candidate Definition
A candidate whose underlying opportunity frequency, margin, and executable capture are so robust that it is designed to operate independently as a full trading strategy (e.g., generating >200 opportunities per year with sufficient expected value).

## 4. Component Candidate Definition
A candidate that is economically and behaviorally promising as an individual artifact, but lacks the opportunity frequency, capital utilization, or broad regime applicability to justify standalone deployment. A **COMPONENT-CANDIDATE** is retained and banked for potential inclusion in a multi-artifact System Assembly.

## 5. Frequency Doctrine
**LOW FREQUENCY IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY A KILL CONDITION.**
Frequency is evaluated relative to the candidate's intended role. A candidate with low standalone frequency but exceptional, scientifically validated per-event economics will be classified as a COMPONENT-CANDIDATE rather than KILLED. No universal minimum frequency threshold exists across all research objects.

## 6. System Opportunity Frequency
System Opportunity Frequency refers to how often the UNION of independently qualified artifacts produces an executable opportunity. Component quality and system frequency are fundamentally separate questions. A robust system may consist of multiple low-frequency, high-expectancy artifacts (e.g., rare macro shocks combined with rare structural traps) that collectively produce a high-frequency opportunity stream.

## 7. System Assembly Gate
A future governed milestone: **SYSTEM ASSEMBLY / MULTI-ARTIFACT ECONOMIC VALIDATION**.
This stage is authorized only after multiple independent artifacts have passed their respective research and scientific gates. System Assembly will evaluate:
- **Opportunity Union:** Combined event frequency.
- **Overlap:** Handling simultaneous or conflicting signals.
- **Correlation:** Dependence among component returns.
- **Transaction Costs:** Aggregate friction and turnover impact.
- **Capital Allocation:** Risk distribution across components.
- **Drawdown:** Combined systemic risk.
- **Diversification:** Evidence of differentiated behavioral capture.
- **OOS / Forward Validation:** System-level performance in holdout and paper execution.

## 8. Combination vs Rescue
**COMBINATION CANNOT RESCUE FAILED RESEARCH.**
Failed or scientifically unsupported candidates (e.g., negative expectancy edges or contradicted hypotheses) cannot be made investable merely by combining them with other failed candidates or burying them in a portfolio. System Assembly is permitted *only* after components have independently earned their scientific and economic validity. Combination is an assembly mechanism, NOT a rescue mechanism.

## 9. System-Level Validation
System composition must be governed and pre-registered prior to system-level validation. System assembly firewalls prohibit:
- Selecting only winning components after seeing historical combination results.
- Arbitrarily optimizing component weights ex-post.
- Endlessly cherry-picking time periods or dropping underperforming artifacts to manufacture a perfect equity curve.

## 10. Capital / Drawdown / Correlation
System Assembly shifts evaluation from individual per-event expectancy to aggregate capital efficiency. The validation must measure the combined drawdown depth and the correlation structure of the assembled components to ensure the system does not silently concentrate risk.

## 11. Forward Validation
Ultimately, the mathematically combined system must undergo and survive formal Forward Validation (G6) to ensure the unified architecture functions in live, unobserved market conditions without degradation.

## 12. Governance Integrity
QuantForge is ultimately searching for a **TRADEABLE SYSTEM**, not merely a single universally active strategy. This governance ensures that high-quality but specialized edge discoveries are banked and utilized correctly, without compromising the strict falsifiability and economic rigor of the V2 Research Factory.
