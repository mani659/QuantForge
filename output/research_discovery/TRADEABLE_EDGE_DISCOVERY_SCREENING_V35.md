## 6. Candidate Proposals

### Candidate 1: CAND-103 — Multi-Timeframe Break Coordination

**Expression Class:** STATE / CONDITION
**Mechanism Family:** Multi-Timeframe Interaction

**Market phenomenon:** When structural breaks occur simultaneously across multiple timeframes (e.g., both a 15-minute high and a 1-hour high break in the same move), the market experiences coordinated forced repositioning across participant groups. This creates a distinct market condition compared to single-timeframe breaks.

**Mechanism:** Different participant groups operate at different timeframes. When a 15-minute break coincides with a 1-hour break, both short-term and medium-term participants are forced to reposition simultaneously. The cumulative effect is greater directional conviction, tighter subsequent distributions, and potentially stronger continuation.

**Observable:** Number of timeframe levels broken in the same directional move (single-timeframe break vs multi-timeframe break).

**Expected economic effect:** Multi-timeframe breaks produce tighter distributions and stronger directional continuation than single-timeframe breaks. The coordination of participant groups creates orderly repricing.

**Why this is genuinely distinct:**
- No prior candidate has tested multi-timeframe coordination
- CAND-077 tested volatility regime STATE, not cross-timeframe break coordination
- CAND-096 tested volatility acceleration, not multi-timeframe break dynamics
- CAND-095 tested shock direction, not cross-timeframe break scope

### Candidate 2: CAND-104 — Post-Magnitude Directional Drift

**Expression Class:** STATE / CONDITION
**Mechanism Family:** Directional Dynamics

**Market phenomenon:** After an unusually large directional move (relative to recent volatility), the market exhibits directional drift — the tendency to continue in the same direction. This is distinct from CAND-095 (which tested shock DIRECTION, not shock MAGNITUDE) and from simple momentum (which uses return-based measures, not volatility-adjusted magnitude).

**Mechanism:** Very large moves trigger forced repositioning: stop-losses, margin calls, and systematic rebalancing. These forced flows take time to complete, creating directional drift in the hours after the initial move. The magnitude of the move determines the volume of forced flows.

**Observable:** Volatility-adjusted move magnitude (move size / recent ATR). Direction of subsequent drift.

**Expected economic effect:** Very large moves (e.g., >3x ATR) produce stronger directional continuation than moderate moves. The economic effect is directional bias proportional to magnitude.

**Why this is genuinely distinct:**
- CAND-095 tested shock DIRECTION (up vs down). CAND-104 tests shock MAGNITUDE (size relative to volatility). Different observable.
- CAND-096 tested volatility ACCELERATION (rate of change). CAND-104 tests move SIZE (absolute magnitude). Different observable.
- Simple momentum uses return-based measures. CAND-104 uses volatility-adjusted magnitude, which normalizes for the market's recent activity level.

## 7. Mechanism–Observable Challenge

### CAND-103: Multi-Timeframe Break Coordination

#### 7.1 Mechanism Independence

**What market phenomenon exists independently of the feature?**

Multi-timeframe analysis is a fundamental concept in market participation. Traders operating at different timeframes (scalpers, day traders, swing traders) all respond to breaks at their respective levels. When breaks coincide across timeframes, these groups respond simultaneously. This phenomenon exists independently of any specific formula.

#### 7.2 Proxy Challenge

**How could the observable arise without the claimed mechanism?**

Multi-timeframe breaks could occur simply because price is moving strongly in one direction — in a trending market, breaks at multiple timeframes happen naturally without any coordination mechanism. The observable could reflect trend strength rather than participant coordination.

#### 7.3 Alternative Explanation

**What competing process could produce the same observable?**

1. **Simple trend strength:** Strong trends naturally break multiple timeframe levels. The observable may capture trend magnitude, not coordination.
2. **Volatility regime:** High-volatility periods produce more breaks at all timeframes. The observable may reflect volatility state.
3. **Look-ahead bias:** If timeframe level definitions are sensitive to future data, the coordination may be an artifact.

#### 7.4 Observable Fidelity

**Why does the observable reasonably measure the mechanism?**

The number of timeframe levels broken in a move directly captures the core hypothesis: cross-timeframe coordination. However, the mechanism (participant coordination) is inferred. The observable captures the structural pattern; the mechanism explains why it matters.

#### 7.5 Expected-Effect Bridge

**Why should that mechanism alter the future outcome distribution?**

If multi-timeframe breaks force coordinated repositioning, the subsequent price action should be more orderly (tighter distributions) and more directional (stronger continuation). The coordination reduces uncertainty about the new price level.

#### 7.6 Falsification

**What observation would contradict the hypothesis?**

If multi-timeframe breaks produce identical distributions to single-timeframe breaks, the hypothesis is contradicted. If multi-timeframe breaks produce WIDER distributions (opposite of predicted), the mechanism explanation is wrong.

#### 7.7 Tradeability Challenge

**Why might any information disappear after realistic costs?**

1. **Timing:** Identifying multi-timeframe breaks requires waiting for the move to complete, which may delay entry.
2. **Frequency:** Multi-timeframe breaks may be rare.
3. **Transaction costs:** If the separation is small, costs consume the effect.

#### 7.8 Base-Rate Challenge

**Why does this deserve G1 given the historically low qualification rate?**

Multi-timeframe interaction is a genuinely unexplored mechanism dimension. No prior candidate has tested cross-timeframe break coordination. The base rate is poor, but the mechanism is economically plausible and the observable is clean.

### CAND-104: Post-Magnitude Directional Drift

#### 7.1 Mechanism Independence

**What market phenomenon exists independently of the feature?**

Post-shock drift is a documented market phenomenon. After large price moves, forced repositioning (stop-losses, margin calls, systematic rebalancing) takes time to complete, creating directional continuation. This exists independently of any specific formula.

#### 7.2 Proxy Challenge

**How could the observable arise without the claimed mechanism?**

Very large moves could occur at the end of a trend (climax), after which the market reverses rather than continues. The observable could capture exhaustion rather than forced repositioning. Additionally, large moves may simply reflect high volatility, and subsequent drift may reflect volatility clustering rather than forced flows.

#### 7.3 Alternative Explanation

**What competing process could produce the same observable?**

1. **Trend climax:** Very large moves may occur at the end of trends, producing reversal rather than continuation.
2. **Volatility clustering:** Large moves cluster with other large moves, creating apparent drift.
3. **Mean reversion:** After extreme moves, prices may revert, producing opposite-direction drift.
4. **Sample composition:** If large moves are rare, the result may be driven by a few outliers.

#### 7.4 Observable Fidelity

**Why does the observable reasonably measure the mechanism?**

Volatility-adjusted move magnitude (move size / ATR) directly captures the core hypothesis: the SIZE of the move relative to recent activity. However, the mechanism (forced repositioning) is inferred. The observable captures the structural pattern; the mechanism explains why it matters.

#### 7.5 Expected-Effect Bridge

**Why should that mechanism alter the future outcome distribution?**

If very large moves trigger forced repositioning that takes time to complete, the subsequent price action should exhibit directional continuation proportional to the move magnitude. The forced flows create persistent directional pressure.

#### 7.6 Falsification

**What observation would contradict the hypothesis?**

If very large moves produce NO directional bias (subsequent returns are random regardless of magnitude), the hypothesis is contradicted. If very large moves produce REVERSAL bias (opposite-direction drift), the mechanism is wrong (though the observable might still be informative).

#### 7.7 Tradeability Challenge

**Why might any information disappear after realistic costs?**

1. **Timing:** Identifying the magnitude requires waiting for the move to complete.
2. **Frequency:** Very large moves (>3x ATR) are rare.
3. **Transaction costs:** If the directional bias is small, costs consume the effect.
4. **Regime dependence:** The effect may exist only in trending periods, not range-bound periods.

#### 7.8 Base-Rate Challenge

**Why does this deserve G1 given the historically low qualification rate?**

CAND-095 tested shock DIRECTION and found no asymmetry. CAND-104 tests shock MAGNITUDE — a different observable class. The forced-repositioning mechanism is economically plausible and the observable is clean. The base rate is poor, but the mechanism is genuinely distinct from prior work.
