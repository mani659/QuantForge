## 7. HYPOTHESIS 01

### Hypothesis ID
**F-01** (formulation-class prefix; NOT a candidate-ledger entry, NOT a Base ID)

### Name
**Session-Partition Reference Process (Overnight Exposure Leg)**

### Classification
Architecture-first / context-decision. Calendar-session architecture with fixed direction. Zero free parameters.

### Decision Process
On every trading day of the declared window, hold long exposure in one liquid US equity index from the prior day's regular-session close to the next day's regular-session open (the overnight leg); be flat from the open to the close (the continuous-session leg). One decision per trading day: whether the process holds the overnight leg.

Eleven-question completeness (BF1/BF6):
1. **Context** — a liquid US equity index with a defined regular session (declared: 09:30–16:00 ET auction-bounded session); daily open/close observations.
2. **Participation** — every trading day; participation is the overnight leg (always-on within the declared window).
3. **Direction** — long (the risk-bearing side of the overnight interval in an equity index; see Mechanism).
4. **Entry** — at the prior day's close, market-on-close semantics.
5. **Exit** — at the next day's open, market-on-open semantics.
6. **Risk / invalidation** — fixed-horizon leg: no open risk beyond the overnight interval. Invalidation is deterministic: if a day lacks a valid close or the following open, the leg is skipped and recorded as a defined no-trade decision.
7. **Execution** — MOC at close / MOO at open, single instrument, full declared notional.
8. **Scope** — formulation-level: one liquid US equity index daily series (instrument identity and validation window are V38A registration decisions; both historical archive and forward-contiguous accrual exist — see Data requirements).
9. **Outcome** — per-leg net log return close(t) → open(t+1) net of declared transition costs; the population mean and distribution over the frozen window.
10. **Cost model** — proposed validation context: 1 bps each way base (2 bps per overnight leg), 5 bps stress. Not yet frozen.
11. **Opportunity population** — every trading day in the declared validation window = one decision; complete, fixed, enumerable in advance.

### Mechanism
During the continuous session, information is processed and risk is passed between participants in a liquid price-discovery market. Across the overnight interval, holders bear exposure to information that arrives while they cannot react, until the opening auction re-prices it. A market that cannot compel anyone to hold overnight risk must compensate those who do; the compensation accrues to the overnight leg. The mechanism's sign claim — that overnight holders are compensated net of costs — is precisely what future validation tests; it is not assumed here.

### Observable
Session clock and auction prints: regular-session open and close prices per trading day.

### Decision Rule
Deterministic calendar rule, zero free parameters: on day t, hold long from close(t) to open(t+1); flat from open(t+1) to close(t+1). No conditioning on any price pattern, volatility state, or historical outcome.

### Execution
Market-on-close entry and market-on-open exit are executable at index level (cash index or governed index-CFD feed). Forward accrual is feasible via the existing MT5 index-CFD feed (daily bars derived from M1) or a governed daily OHLC source; source governance is a registration-time decision.

### Opportunity Population
All trading days in the declared validation window, each carrying exactly one decision (overnight leg held / no-trade skip). Fixed and enumerable before any measurement — the reference population required by V38A for later Base-vs-Conditional attribution.

### Scope
Formulation-level (NOT frozen): one liquid US equity index; intended validation context is forward-contiguous daily data so that validation evidence is fresh (the archive window, including the H01 motivating window, can never be validating evidence under Motivation ≠ Validation). Data requirements: daily open **and** close observations (FRED close-only series are insufficient for the open leg; the intended source is a governed daily OHLC feed such as MT5 index-CFD M1-derived daily bars — feasibility verified, governance deferred to registration).

### Outcome
Per-leg net log return close(t) → open(t+1), minus declared transition costs, over the frozen window.

### Cost Model
Proposed validation context, not yet frozen: 1 bps entry + 1 bps exit base; 5 bps stress. One coherent cost model, frozen once at registration (V38A).

### Falsification Conditions
1. Mechanism failure: over the validated window, net-of-cost overnight-leg returns show no positive central tendency (indistinguishable from zero or negative) — overnight holders are not compensated.
2. Execution impossibility: close/open fills at declared cost cannot be achieved in the declared environment.
3. Decision-path incoherence: leg outcomes are dominated by index-specific structure rather than the session partition (the partition explains nothing).

No profitability threshold is predefined; these are mechanism and coherence conditions only.

### Distinctiveness
Decision object: session-partition exposure (calendar structure), NOT a price pattern, volatility state, or event. Information dimension: session clock. Execution relationship: daily MOC/MOO, full-day horizon. Conditional structure: none — fully specified now. Attribution architecture: one decision per day, clean fixed population. Vs H01: H01 is close-to-close full-day exposure conditioned on an 11-day-block volatility state; F-01 has no conditioning and partitions the day — a different decision object and information dimension. Vs ORD: intraday opening-range breakout, different horizon/trigger/execution. Vs V27–V36: M1 microstructure event deltas, different object class. Vs CAND-075: an event-occurrence measurement of the closing auction, not a session-leg decision process. F-01 is not a threshold, timeframe, or parameter variant of any of them.

### Historical-Provenance Firewall
Reused: nothing from H01's trigger, tercile, block rule, or costs; no parameter inherited from any archive process. Used as constraints: intraday cost-heavy execution (ORD), M1 event-family exhaustion (V27–V36), vol-state conditioning (CAND-077/107), and the BH-01 withdrawal itself (no outcome-descended trigger). Used as feasibility facts: daily index data availability and the forward feed. No repository outcome is a reason this process exists.

### No-Rescue Declaration
F-01 is coherent and worthy of independent evaluation with no Conditional ever created (BF10): it is a complete, executable, interpretable reference decision process whose economics will be measured on its own. A future Conditional (e.g., state-conditioned overnight legs) would be an increment over this reference — never a requirement for its coherence.

### Research Value
- Strategic: provides the assembled architecture's first candidate reference with a maximally clean fixed daily population.
- Attribution: one decision per day, zero parameters — the cleanest possible Base-vs-Conditional comparison structure.
- Execution: daily, liquid, low-cost; executable in the declared environment.
- Distinctiveness: introduces the session-partition decision class, never formulated in QuantForge.
- Unresolved system question addressed: whether session structure, not price pattern, can anchor a legitimate reference decision process.
- Research-capital efficiency: highest of the three — single instrument, single rule, no search space.

## 8. HYPOTHESIS 02

### Hypothesis ID
**F-02** (formulation-class prefix; NOT a candidate-ledger entry, NOT a Base ID)

### Name
**Two-Index Relative-Value Process (Spread Reversion)**

### Classification
Architecture-first / cross-sectional. Pairs (relative-value) architecture with convergence mechanism. Four conventional design constants declared prospectively; no outcome fitting.

### Decision Process
Maintain a daily long/short book on two liquid US equity indices. Direction is determined by the standardized deviation of the log-price ratio from its recent mean: when the ratio is unusually high, hold the relatively expensive index short and the relatively cheap index long; when unusually low, the opposite; flat otherwise.

Eleven-question completeness (BF1/BF6):
1. **Context** — two highly correlated liquid US equity index daily series (formulation-level: S&P 500 × Nasdaq-100; the pair identity is declared and frozen at registration — the mechanism is pair-agnostic).
2. **Participation** — daily; the process is always in a defined state (flat or positioned).
3. **Direction** — sign of the standardized ratio deviation: z ≥ +1.0 → long the second index, short the first; z ≤ −1.0 → long the first, short the second; otherwise hold current position until the exit rule.
4. **Entry** — at the close of the day the z-score first crosses an entry bound (±1.0).
5. **Exit** — at the close of the day the z-score crosses zero (position closed); or on invalidation (below).
6. **Risk / invalidation** — if |z| exceeds 3.0, the position is closed as a structural-break exit (the deviation is treated as regime change, not mispricing), with a deterministic cooling rule: no new entry until z crosses through zero. Data-gap handling is deterministic (skip day, record no-trade).
7. **Execution** — daily market-on-close, equal declared notional on each leg, one long and one short.
8. **Scope** — formulation-level: two liquid US equity index daily series (repository archive has ≥4 contemporary series: SP500, DJIA, NASDAQ100, NASDAQCOM — all READY in the H01 input manifest); validation window and instrument pair frozen at registration.
9. **Outcome** — daily net log return of the long/short book minus declared leg costs.
10. **Cost model** — proposed validation context: 1 bps per leg per side base (4 bps full round trip), 5 bps stress. Not yet frozen.
11. **Opportunity population** — every trading day in the declared window; each day the process is in a deterministically defined state (flat/positioned), and entry/exit events are the z-crossings; complete and enumerable in advance.

### Mechanism
Two near-substitute indices sharing a common factor (broad US equity exposure) experience transient relative demand shocks — index-specific flows, rebalancing, sentiment — that push their price ratio away from its recent relationship. Because the assets are substitutes, arbitrage activity corrects relative mispricing: the ratio is stationary in expectation even though levels are not. Deviations beyond the recent dispersion are transitory and converge toward the zero-crossing. The claim of convergence net of costs is what future validation tests.

### Observable
The rolling standardized score (z-score) of the log-price ratio over a trailing 20-trading-day window (sample mean and standard deviation of the ratio over the window).

### Decision Rule
Deterministic, declared prospectively: compute z_t = (ratio_t − mean_20(ratio)) / sd_20(ratio). Entry at |z| ≥ 1.0 in the direction of the deviation; exit at z = 0 (zero-crossing); invalidation exit at |z| > 3.0 followed by the cooling rule. Design constants: lookback 20 trading days; entry bound 1.0; exit bound 0.0; invalidation bound 3.0 — conventional statistical choices (one-month standardization, one-sigma deviation, zero reversion point, three-sigma break), fixed at formulation, not fitted to any outcome.

### Execution
Daily market-on-close, equal notional per leg, long one index and short the other; positions reset only on exit rules. Executable at index level (both legs liquid daily index instruments).

### Opportunity Population
All trading days in the frozen window: each day the process is in a defined state; entry/exit events are deterministic functions of price history. Complete, fixed, enumerable before measurement.

### Scope
Formulation-level (NOT frozen): two liquid US equity index daily series; intended validation context is forward-contiguous daily data (fresh evidence; the motivating archive can never be validating evidence). Data requirements: two daily close series — available in the repository archive (FRED public-domain series READY; sp/NYA subject to the recorded Norgate archival governance, resolved at registration) and via forward daily accrual.

### Outcome
Daily net log return of the equal-notional long/short book, minus declared leg costs, over the frozen window.

### Cost Model
Proposed validation context, not yet frozen: 1 bps per leg per side base (4 bps full round trip), 5 bps stress. One coherent cost model, frozen once at registration.

### Falsification Conditions
1. Mechanism failure: deviations beyond ±1σ do not converge to the zero-crossing net of costs over the validated window (no reversion to the relative relationship).
2. Decision-path incoherence: the ratio exhibits non-stationary drift such that the standardized deviation is structurally undefined.
3. Instability: the entry base rate is degenerate (too few entries for a coherent population).
4. Execution impossibility: the two-leg book cannot be executed at declared cost in the declared environment.

No profitability threshold is predefined.

### Distinctiveness
Decision object: a long/short relative book on a pair ratio — NOT a single-asset signal, event, or state. Information dimension: cross-sectional relative price (two-asset), not absolute displacement, volatility, or session structure. Execution relationship: daily close, two-leg equal notional. Conditional structure: none — fully specified now. Attribution architecture: the spread book separates relative pricing from market direction by construction. Vs DISC-021/CAND-097 (closed single-asset reversion): the economic mechanism differs — relative mispricing between substitutes vs absolute overreaction of one price; the decision object (pair book) and information dimension (relative ratio) differ. Vs CAND-076/079 (closed cross-market directional lines): those tested directional spillover exposure; F-02 is a convergence book, a different decision object. Vs F-01: calendar/session partition vs relative-pricing convergence — different mechanism, different information dimension, different decision object.

### Historical-Provenance Firewall
Reused: nothing. No parameter, threshold, pair, or scope was chosen from any archive outcome. Used as constraints: single-asset reversion closure (DISC-021/CAND-097 — do not re-express as outright reversion), cross-market directional closures (CAND-076/079 — do not re-express as directional spillover), intraday cost failures (ORD). Used as feasibility facts: ≥4 daily index series available in both historical and contemporary archives. No repository outcome is a reason this process exists.

### No-Rescue Declaration
F-02 is coherent and worthy of independent evaluation with no Conditional ever created (BF10): the pairs book is a complete reference decision process whose convergence claim is tested on its own economics. A future Conditional (e.g., state-gated entry on top of the reference) would be an increment, never a requirement.

### Research Value
- Strategic: introduces the relative-value decision class — the only major decision class absent from QuantForge's tested universe — as a candidate reference.
- Attribution: a clean fixed daily population with deterministic entry/exit events; the long/short structure isolates relative pricing.
- Execution: daily, liquid, executable; short-leg feasibility is a registration-time realism check, not assumed.
- Distinctiveness: genuinely new decision object and information dimension vs every closed family.
- Unresolved system question addressed: whether relative pricing between substitute indices can anchor a legitimate reference decision process.

## 9. HYPOTHESIS 03

### Hypothesis ID
**F-03** (formulation-class prefix; NOT a candidate-ledger entry, NOT a Base ID)

### Name
**Cross-Sectional Relative-Strength Rotation Process**

### Classification
Architecture-first / cross-sectional. Rank-based multi-index allocation with persistence mechanism. One design constant declared prospectively; no outcome fitting.

### Decision Process
Daily, rank the declared set of liquid US equity index series by trailing 20-trading-day log return; hold the top-ranked index long and the bottom-ranked index short, equal notional, for the next trading day; re-rank and rebalance daily at the close.

Eleven-question completeness (BF1/BF6):
1. **Context** — N ≥ 3 liquid US equity index daily series (formulation-level: S&P 500, Dow Jones Industrial Average, Nasdaq-100, Nasdaq Composite — all present in the repository's contemporary archive).
2. **Participation** — every trading day; the process is always in a defined state.
3. **Direction** — rank-determined: top rank long, bottom rank short; deterministic, no thresholds.
4. **Entry** — at the close of day t, positions set per the day-t ranking.
5. **Exit** — at the close of day t+1, when the next ranking re-sets positions (fixed one-day holding; exit is the daily rebalance).
6. **Risk / invalidation** — daily rebalance bounds exposure to one day. Deterministic guard: if any declared series lacks a valid daily observation, the process is flat that day and the day is recorded as a defined no-trade decision.
7. **Execution** — daily market-on-close, equal notional on each leg, one long, one short.
8. **Scope** — formulation-level: four liquid US equity index daily series (contemporary archive READY per the H01 input manifest); validation window and instrument set frozen at registration.
9. **Outcome** — daily net log return of the top-long/bottom-short book minus declared leg costs.
10. **Cost model** — proposed validation context: 1 bps per leg per side base (4 bps per full daily turnover round trip), 5 bps stress. Daily full turnover is a deliberately high-cost structure; the declared cost convention is a realism the validation will exercise, not a selection reason. Not yet frozen.
11. **Opportunity population** — every trading day in the declared window = one decision (which legs, which sides); complete and enumerable in advance.

### Mechanism
Index-level relative demand persists over weekly/monthly horizons: passive allocation flows, benchmark rebalancing, and institutional/retail demand chase the relatively stronger complex while the relatively weaker sheds demand. The cross-sectional *ranking* captures relative demand differences that own-history measures blur; the top-versus-bottom book expresses that difference directly. The claim of cross-sectional persistence net of costs is what future validation tests.

### Observable
Trailing 20-trading-day log returns ranked across the declared index set.

### Decision Rule
Deterministic rank rule with one design constant (20-trading-day lookback — a conventional one-month calibration-free choice, declared prospectively, not fitted): on day t, long the highest-return series, short the lowest-return series, equal notional, at the close; reverse/rebalance at the next close.

### Execution
Daily market-on-close equal-notional long/short across two of the declared instruments; full turnover daily. Executable at index level with liquid daily instruments.

### Opportunity Population
All trading days in the frozen window: each day exactly one decision (legs and sides are deterministic functions of price history). Complete, fixed, enumerable before measurement.

### Scope
Formulation-level (NOT frozen): four liquid US equity index daily series; intended validation context is forward-contiguous daily data (fresh evidence; the motivating archive can never be validating evidence). Data requirements: four daily close series — available in the repository archive (contemporary FRED series READY; NYA/sp subject to the recorded Norgate archival governance, resolved at registration) and via forward daily accrual.

### Outcome
Daily net log return of the top-long/bottom-short book, minus declared leg costs, over the frozen window.

### Cost Model
Proposed validation context, not yet frozen: 1 bps per leg per side base (4 bps per daily turnover round trip), 5 bps stress. One coherent cost model, frozen once at registration.

### Falsification Conditions
1. Mechanism failure: rank-based relative returns are not distinguishable from zero net of costs over the validated window (no cross-sectional persistence).
2. Attribution failure: the book's returns are dominated by a common factor — the relative structure explains nothing.
3. Execution impossibility: daily full turnover cannot be executed at the declared cost in the declared environment.
4. Instability: the rank structure churns such that the population of decisions is incoherent.

No profitability threshold is predefined.

### Distinctiveness
Decision object: a rank-ordered multi-asset book — NOT a single-asset signal, event, or state. Information dimension: the cross-section of relative returns across ≥3 assets — NOT an own-history path. Execution relationship: daily full-turnover rebalance. Conditional structure: none — fully specified now. Attribution architecture: the top/bottom book is constructed to isolate relative strength from market direction. Vs DISC-022 TSMOM (closed): single-asset own-history trend vs cross-sectional ranking — different information dimension and decision object; this is not a TSMOM re-expression. Vs H01: the archive's six markets were used only as an equal-weight basket; they were never ranked against each other — F-03 uses the same data dimension differently. Vs F-02: convergence (spread reversion, pair) vs persistence (ranking rotation, multi-asset); opposite economic claims, different decision objects. Vs F-01: single-asset session partition vs multi-asset rank allocation.

### Historical-Provenance Firewall
Reused: nothing. No lookback, instrument set, or ranking scheme was chosen from any archive outcome. Used as constraints: TSMOM closure (DISC-022 — do not re-express as own-history momentum), single-asset reversion closure, intraday cost failures (ORD), M1 microstructure exhaustion (V27–V36). Used as feasibility facts: four contemporary daily index series READY in the archive. No repository outcome is a reason this process exists.

### No-Rescue Declaration
F-03 is coherent and worthy of independent evaluation with no Conditional ever created (BF10): the rank book is a complete reference decision process tested on its own economics. A future Conditional (e.g., regime-gated rotation on top of the reference) would be an increment, never a requirement.

### Research Value
- Strategic: introduces the cross-sectional decision class; exercises the multi-index daily archive in a way never attempted (H01 used the markets only as an equal-weight basket).
- Attribution: clean fixed daily population; the long/short structure isolates relative strength.
- Execution: daily, liquid, executable; daily turnover makes the cost realism explicit and testable.
- Distinctiveness: new information dimension (cross-section) and decision object (rank book).
- Unresolved system question addressed: whether cross-sectional relative demand, distinct from own-history trend, can anchor a legitimate reference decision process.
